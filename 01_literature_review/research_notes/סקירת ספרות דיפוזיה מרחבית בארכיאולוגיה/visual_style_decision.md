# Visual Output Style for a Text → Layout → Diffusion Archaeological Reconstruction Pipeline: Photorealistic vs Schematic

Scope: evidence for choosing the output style of a pipeline (excavation-report text → LLM spatial layout → layout-conditioned diffusion such as ControlNet/GLIGEN → CV evaluation of layout faithfulness), judged on (1) archaeological/epistemic validity, (2) technical feasibility, (3) CV measurability. Compiled 2026-11-14. Verification markers: **[primary, read]** = primary text opened and quoted in this session; **[abstract/snippet]** = only abstract, metadata or search snippet seen; **[unverified]** = could not open, treat with caution.

## Q1. What do archaeological standards and theory say about visual reconstruction, transparency, paradata, uncertainty and photorealism (incl. 2023–2026 critiques of AI "pasts")?

### Takeaway
The London Charter does not ban any style, but it does three things. It names the choice between "more or less photo-realistic, impressionistic or schematic" as a decision that must be evaluated and documented (2.3, 4.7). It requires users to be told whether an image shows the existing state, an evidence-based restoration or a hypothetical reconstruction, along with "the extent and nature of any factual uncertainty" (4.4). It also requires paradata (4.6). The Seville Principles add the requirement to "show the different levels of accuracy" (4.4.2). The NPR and virtual-archaeology literature since 1996–1999 argues consistently that photorealism suggests certainty the evidence does not support, and that abstraction or schematic rendering signals hypothesis. 2023–2026 critiques of generative AI images add the specific risks of bias, outdated science and blended hallucination.

### Cited Findings

**London Charter (Draft 2.1, 7 Feb 2009, ed. Hugh Denard) [primary, read]**
- Preamble: research outcomes that use computer-based visualisation "should accurately convey to users the status of the knowledge that they represent, such as distinctions between evidence and hypothesis, and between different levels of probability." — [London Charter 2.1 PDF](https://www.londoncharter.org/fileadmin/templates/main/docs/london_charter_2_1_en.pdf)
- Principle 2 (Aims and Methods): "A computer-based visualisation method should normally be used only when it is the most appropriate available method for that purpose"; 2.2 requires "A systematic, documented evaluation of the suitability of each method to each aim". — [London Charter 2.1](https://www.londoncharter.org/fileadmin/templates/main/docs/london_charter_2_1_en.pdf)
- **2.3 names the exact decision this team faces:** "the choice of computer-based visualisation method (e.g. more or less photo-realistic, impressionistic or schematic; representation of hypotheses or of the available evidence; dynamic or static) … should be based on an evaluation of the likely success of each approach in addressing each aim." — [London Charter 2.1](https://www.londoncharter.org/fileadmin/templates/main/docs/london_charter_2_1_en.pdf)
- 3.3: "Particular attention should be given to the way in which visual sources may be affected by ideological, historical, social, religious and aesthetic and other such factors." — [London Charter 2.1](https://www.londoncharter.org/fileadmin/templates/main/docs/london_charter_2_1_en.pdf)
- **4.4 (Documentation of Knowledge Claims):** "It should be made clear to users what a computer-based visualisation seeks to represent, for example the existing state, an evidence-based restoration or an hypothetical reconstruction of a cultural heritage object or site, and the extent and nature of any factual uncertainty." — [London Charter 2.1](https://www.londoncharter.org/fileadmin/templates/main/docs/london_charter_2_1_en.pdf)
- **4.6 (Documentation of Process, "Paradata"):** "Documentation of the evaluative, analytical, deductive, interpretative and creative decisions made in the course of computer-based visualisation should be disseminated in such a way that the relationship between research sources, implicit knowledge, explicit reasoning, and visualisation-based outcomes can be understood." — [London Charter 2.1](https://www.londoncharter.org/fileadmin/templates/main/docs/london_charter_2_1_en.pdf)
- **4.7:** "The rationale for choosing a computer-based visualisation method, and for rejecting other methods, should be documented and disseminated". 4.10: hypothetical dependency relationships between elements should be "clearly identified by users and the reasoning underlying such hypotheses understood". 4.11: documentation may be "graphical, textual, video, audio, numerical or combinations". — [London Charter 2.1](https://www.londoncharter.org/fileadmin/templates/main/docs/london_charter_2_1_en.pdf)
- Glossary, **Paradata**: "Information about human processes of understanding and interpretation of data objects", e.g., "descriptions stored within a structured dataset of how evidence was used to interpret an artefact". **Intellectual transparency**: "The provision of information, presented in any medium or format, to allow users to understand the nature and scope of 'knowledge claim' made by a computer-based visualisation outcome." — [London Charter 2.1](https://www.londoncharter.org/fileadmin/templates/main/docs/london_charter_2_1_en.pdf)
- The short principles web page gives only the six top-level principles. The detailed clauses above come from the 2.1 PDF. — [londoncharter.org/principles](https://www.londoncharter.org/principles.html)

**Seville Principles (International Principles of Virtual Archaeology) [primary text of the 2011 version, read]**
- Text read: López-Menchero & Grande, "The Principles of the Seville Charter" (CIPA paper; PDF metadata dated 30 May 2011). It states that the London Charter is its theoretical framework. — [López-Menchero & Grande 2011](https://www.cipaheritagedocumentation.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/12/López-Menchero-Grande-The-principles-of-the-Seville-Charter.pdf)
- **Principle 4, Authenticity:** visualisation "reconstructs or recreates historical buildings and environments as we believe them to have been in the past. For that reason, it should always be possible to distinguish what is real, genuine or authentic from what is not." 4.4.1: alternative interpretations should be shown when they have equal scientific validity, otherwise "only the main hypothesis will be endorsed." — [López-Menchero & Grande 2011](https://www.cipaheritagedocumentation.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/12/López-Menchero-Grande-The-principles-of-the-Seville-Charter.pdf)
- **4.4.2:** reconstructions "must explicitly or through additional interpretations show the different levels of accuracy on which the restoration or reconstruction is based." — [López-Menchero & Grande 2011](https://www.cipaheritagedocumentation.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/12/López-Menchero-Grande-The-principles-of-the-Seville-Charter.pdf)
- **4.4.3:** visualisation should help differentiate between remains conserved "in situ", remains returned to position ("real anastylosis"), areas rebuilt on original remains, and areas "restored or reconstructed virtually". This is effectively a four-level legend. — [López-Menchero & Grande 2011](https://www.cipaheritagedocumentation.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/12/López-Menchero-Grande-The-principles-of-the-Seville-Charter.pdf)
- **Counter-pressure in Principle 5, Historical rigour:** 4.5.2 rejects "an idyllic image of the past with seemingly newly-constructed buildings, people who look like models". 4.5.3 says reconstructions "cannot systematically show lifeless cities, lonely buildings or dead landscapes, because this is an historical falsehood." — [López-Menchero & Grande 2011](https://www.cipaheritagedocumentation.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/12/López-Menchero-Grande-The-principles-of-the-Seville-Charter.pdf)
- Principle 7, Scientific transparency: visualisation "must be essentially transparent, i.e. testable by other researchers". 7.1 requires documenting "objectives, methodology, techniques, reasoning, origin and characteristics of the sources". — [López-Menchero & Grande 2011](https://www.cipaheritagedocumentation.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/12/López-Menchero-Grande-The-principles-of-the-Seville-Charter.pdf)
- Principle 8: 8.3 says research-oriented visualisations should be evaluated by "testing by a sufficiently representative number of end users". **8.4: "The final quality of any computer-based visualisation must be measured by the rigour with which it has been developed and not the spectacularity of its results."** — [López-Menchero & Grande 2011](https://www.cipaheritagedocumentation.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/12/López-Menchero-Grande-The-principles-of-the-Seville-Charter.pdf)
- A 2025 Eurographics DH paper says ICOMOS ratified the Seville Principles in 2017 as a code of best practice, and proposes a "Seal of Compliance" to separate source-based reconstructions from artistic or speculative renderings. **[abstract/snippet; full text not opened; the ICOMOS General Assembly location and session are unverified]** — [Project to Operationalize the Seville Principles (EG DH 2025)](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.2312/dh20253007)

### Inferences
- The London Charter is style-neutral, but it puts the burden of proof on the team. Whatever style is chosen, 2.2, 2.3 and 4.7 require a documented comparison and a documented reason for rejecting the other option. This decision memo is itself part of compliance.
- Clauses 4.4 and 4.10, and Seville 4.4.2 and 4.4.3, require per-element status (in situ / restored / inferred / hypothetical) to be **visible or attached**. Photorealistic rendering can only meet this by adding overlays such as ghosting, colour tints or legends, which breaks the photographic look. A schematic style can encode it natively in line type, fill saturation or hatching. This is the main epistemic argument for schematic output.
- The pipeline's LLM step is a layer of interpretation between the excavation text and the image. London 4.6 (paradata) therefore implies that each layout element should carry its source sentence or span from the report and an evidence class. A schematic style can show that class directly.
- Seville 4.5.2 and 4.5.3 caution against sterile, "lifeless" or idealised depictions. That is a warning about content (phases, decay, people, landscape), not a mandate for photorealism, but it is a fair criticism of overly clean CAD-like schematics. It can be partly addressed by depicting phases and decay states as schematic layers.
- The 2023 and 2026 AAP studies are the strongest recent evidence that text-to-image priors carry outdated or biased "pasts". Photorealistic rendering amplifies whatever the prior hallucinates and makes it hard to tell apart from evidence (Morgan). A tightly layout-conditioned schematic leaves less room for the prior to add unrequested content such as furniture, people and technologies.

- The final 2011 Seville text and the ICOMOS 2017 adoption document were not opened directly. The quoted clauses come from the 2011 López-Menchero & Grande paper, whose numbering (4.4.x) is the paper's section numbering. Clause wording may differ slightly in the final version.

- No 2024–2026 paper was found that specifically critiques *layout-conditioned* (ControlNet/GLIGEN) archaeological reconstructions. The critiques found concern free text-to-image generation.

## Q2. Technical feasibility: how well do diffusion + ControlNet/LoRA produce line-art / technical / archaeological-illustration styles vs photorealistic scenes, and what data exists?

### Takeaway
Photorealism is the native and best-supported regime. GLIGEN and ControlNet were trained and benchmarked on photo datasets (COCO, ADE20K), so photoreal output works off the shelf. Schematic and line-art output is feasible: ControlNet 1.1 ships lineart, MLSD (straight lines), scribble and colour-coded segmentation models; community LoRAs exist for axonometric, floorplan and section styles; and a 2025 diffusion model produces publication-standard archaeological pottery drawings. Two costs remain. Diffusion models often produce drawings that look plausible but are structurally wrong, and there is no public corpus of archaeological reconstruction drawings, so a style LoRA must be trained on self-rendered or licensed data.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- **Photorealistic:** this needs the least engineering, since COCO/ADE20K-trained ControlNet-seg and GLIGEN work as is. But the photographic prior will add plausible detail (materials, clutter, lighting, people) that no layout element or report sentence supports. That is a feasibility advantage and an epistemic liability at the same time.
- **Schematic:** feasible with (a) ControlNet-seg using a custom palette (manual masks are accepted) plus lineart/MLSD conditioning for edges, and (b) a style LoRA. The LoRA training data problem can be solved procedurally. Render the team's own synthetic layouts as axonometric or plan line-art with flat class fills (e.g., Blender Freestyle or a simple 2D/2.5D rasteriser). This gives unlimited, copyright-clean, *paired* (layout, image) data. Supplement it with a few public-domain or licensed reconstruction drawings for stylistic flavour.
- Choose **axonometric/isometric (parallel projection)** or **plan view**, not perspective. Under parallel projection the mapping from the layout's ground plane to the image is a fixed affine transform, so ground-truth boxes and masks can be projected exactly into image space. Plan view is the simplest and matches the excavation-plan genre. Axonometric is the classic reconstruction-drawing view and still keeps the geometry deterministic. *(geometric inference)*
- The known weakness (structurally wrong lines) argues for keeping ControlNet conditioning strong and treating line straightness and closure as measured quantities, not assuming them.

### Gaps
- No benchmark was found that quantifies ControlNet or GLIGEN layout faithfulness when the *output* style is line-art (as opposed to line-art *inputs*).
- Civitai originals of the LoRAs were not opened. Licences, base-model versions and training sources should be checked before use.
- Data requirements for few-shot style fine-tuning (DreamBooth: 3–5 subject images, arXiv 2208.12242; StyleDrop: style from a single image, arXiv 2306.00983) come from prior knowledge and were **not re-verified in this session**.
- No dataset of *photographs of reconstructed archaeological sites* suitable for layout-paired training was found. Such photos would show modern reconstructions, which is itself epistemically problematic.

## Q3. Measurability: how do photo-trained CV evaluators perform on sketches, line drawings and illustrations, and which style makes layout faithfulness (mAP vs boxes, mIoU vs masks) more reliably measurable? Is there a hybrid?

### Takeaway
Off-the-shelf detectors and segmenters lose roughly half or more of their accuracy on non-photographic domains. Examples: Clipart1k mAP 26.8 vs 55.4 with in-domain training; PACS sketch 54% vs photo 87.7%; Grounding DINO AP 0.26 on sketches vs 0.74 after fine-tuning. A plain line-art output measured with off-the-shelf YOLO, Grounding DINO or SAM would therefore be **less** reliably measurable than a photoreal one. Photoreal evaluation has its own ceiling, though: OneFormer gets only 0.58 IoU even on real ADE20K ground truth, and reported metrics are "upper-bounded by the accuracy of the condition detector". A *schematic with palette-locked semantic colour fills* removes the learned evaluator for mIoU entirely (colour quantisation becomes the segmenter), and a small domain-specific detector can be trained on synthetic renders, as LayoutBench does. That makes it the most measurable option *if designed for measurement*.

### Cited Findings
- **Cross-domain detection (Inoue et al., CVPR 2018) [primary HTML, read]:** detectors trained on PASCAL VOC and tested on Clipart1k, without adaptation: SSD300 26.8 mAP (ideal in-domain fine-tune 55.4), YOLOv2 25.5 (51.2), Faster R-CNN 26.2 (50.0). Watercolor2k: SSD300 49.6 (58.4). Comic2k: 24.9 (46.4). "Using an object detector that is neither trained nor fine-tuned for the target domain causes a significant drop in performance." The detector "mainly fails because of confusion with other classes and backgrounds rather than inaccurate localization." — [Inoue et al. 2018](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1803.11365)
- **DomainNet (Peng et al., ICCV 2019) [primary HTML, read]:** single-best source-only accuracy is real 41.6, clipart 39.6, painting 33.9, sketch 23.1, quickdraw 11.8, infograph 8.2. ResNet-101 oracle: real 80.1, clipart 69.3, sketch 60.7, quickdraw 66.8, infograph 34.5. The authors call the dataset "challenging, especially for the infograph and quickdraw domain". — [Peng et al. 2019](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1812.01754)
- **PACS (Li et al., ICCV 2017) [primary HTML, read]:** held-out-domain accuracy (AlexNet Deep-All): photo 87.70, art 63.30, cartoon 63.13, **sketch 54.07**. "Sketch is the most different from the other three domains." — [Li et al. 2017](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1710.03077)
- **Open-vocabulary detection and SAM on sketches (Tang, Vinker, Yan, Zhang & Agrawala, arXiv Feb/May 2025) [primary HTML, read]:** zero-shot Grounding DINO on scene sketches averaged AP 0.26 / AP50 0.37 across 8 datasets, against 0.74 / 0.85 after class-agnostic fine-tuning; on SketchyScene, 0.24 vs 0.83 AP. "The domain gap between real and sketched objects presents significant challenges for Grounding DINO." SAM "exhibits a surprising ability to generalize to sketches" but "may miss sketch object boundaries or produce grid-like masks". Grounded SAM reached IoU 0.32 and automatic SAM IoU 0.08, against 0.82 for the adapted method. — [arXiv 2502.09608v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.09608v2)
- **CLIP robustness:** zero-shot CLIP shrinks the "robustness gap" under natural distribution shifts, including ImageNet-Sketch and ImageNet-R, "by up to 75%" relative to ImageNet-trained models of equal ImageNet accuracy. The benefit "is almost entirely gone" after supervised fine-tuning. **[exact per-dataset figure values not extracted]** — [CLIP, arXiv 2103.00020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.00020). A 2024 benchmark (RoZ) finds zero-shot CLIP is not significantly more robust than ImageNet models in most *synthetic* shift settings. — [arXiv 2403.10499](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.10499v1)
- **Evaluator ceiling on photoreal outputs (ControlNet, ICCV 2023) [primary HTML, read]:** segmentation-conditioned generation was evaluated by re-segmenting outputs with OneFormer. IoU: **ADE20K ground-truth images 0.58 ± 0.10**, ControlNet 0.35 ± 0.14, ControlNet-lite 0.32, LDM 0.31, PITI 0.26, VQGAN 0.21. — [ControlNet arXiv 2302.05543](https://arxiv.org/html/2302.05543v3)
- **Cycle-consistency controllability metrics (ControlNet++, ECCV 2024):** segmentation is scored by mIoU, Canny by F1, **HED and LineArt by SSIM between input and re-extracted edge maps**, and depth by RMSE. Reported gains over ControlNet are mIoU +7.9 (v1; 11.1 in v3), SSIM +13.4%, RMSE +7.6%. **[abstract/snippet; figures are version-dependent]** — [ControlNet++ arXiv 2404.07987](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07987)
- **Caveats on such metrics (CPO, arXiv Nov 2025):** evaluation is "upper-bounded by the accuracy of the condition detector". Implementation differences (Kornia vs OpenCV Canny thresholds) "can lead to inconsistencies". Lineart and Canny gains are smallest, attributed to "sparsity of supervision in the latent space". **[snippet]** — [CPO arXiv 2511.04753](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.04753)
- **Domain-specific evaluator precedent (LayoutBench, Cho et al., CVPRW 2024) [primary HTML, read]:** layout accuracy is measured as detector AP on generated images. The detector is DETR-R101-DC5 trained "on separately generated 5K training images for each of 8 tasks, with 40K total images" in the synthetic CLEVR domain "to better capture the objects with uncommon sizes, positions, and aspect ratios" and "to disentangle the factor of image generation quality from the interested spatial controllability". Ground-truth renders score 60.5 / 93.5 AP/AP50 (CLEVR val). LDM scores 54.5 / 91.8 in-distribution but **9.9 / 29.0 on out-of-distribution layouts**; IterInpaint scores 36.5 / 64.1. The real-object variant (LayoutBench-COCO) uses YOLOv7. — [LayoutBench](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2304.06671)
- GLIGEN's box-faithfulness on photoreal COCO is modest in absolute terms (YOLO AP 22.4). — [GLIGEN](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.07093)

### Inferences
- **Plain line-art + off-the-shelf evaluators = worst measurability.** The evidence predicts roughly 50% relative AP loss from domain shift alone. That loss would mix with the generator's real errors, so a low mAP could not be attributed to the generator.
- **Photoreal + off-the-shelf evaluators = moderate measurability.** These are the standard protocols (YOLO AP, OneFormer mIoU) and results are comparable with the GLIGEN and ControlNet papers. But even ground-truth photos reach only ~0.58 IoU, and archaeological classes (posthole, hearth, pit, threshold, rampart) are absent from COCO and ADE20K. Open-vocabulary detectors would then be operating out of distribution in *class*, if not in style. LayoutBench shows that layouts which are out of distribution for the generator (typical for unusual archaeological plans) cut AP sharply.
- **Evidence-coded schematic with palette-locked fills = best measurability, by design.** If each layout class is rendered as a flat fill from a fixed palette (and evidence level as line style or saturation), then:
  - **mIoU**: nearest-palette classification in a perceptual colour space (e.g., CIELAB with a tolerance) gives a deterministic segmentation, so no learned evaluator and no 0.58-type ceiling apply. Masks are compared with the layout projected through the fixed axonometric or plan transform.
  - **Box mAP / IoU**: connected components per palette class give instance boxes, compared with the input boxes.
  - **Line fidelity**: re-extract lineart or MLSD and score SSIM/F1 against the conditioning edges (ControlNet++ protocol). Line-art extractors applied to line-art images are close to the identity, which removes much of the domain gap.
  - **Learned-detector cross-check**: fine-tune a detector (e.g., YOLO or Grounding DINO) on procedurally rendered schematic images, following LayoutBench. The 0.26 → 0.74 AP jump in Tang et al. shows such adaptation is effective.
  - **Calibration**: report every metric on ground-truth renders as well, as ControlNet's GT row and LayoutBench's GT row do, so that evaluator error and generator error are separated.
- **Circularity risk:** if the segmentation map is both the condition and (in colour) the output, colour mIoU partly measures "copy-through". Mitigations: condition some element classes only by GLIGEN boxes and text, so the model must synthesise their shape; measure added or hallucinated content (palette pixels outside any layout element); and report line, perspective and style quality separately. *(inference)*
- **Practical risk:** diffusion VAEs do not reproduce exact RGB values, so palette colours must be well separated, and quantisation tolerance should be validated on ground-truth renders passed through VAE encode/decode. *(inference, untested here)*
- **Hybrid "generate photoreal, evaluate via edge/seg maps"** is possible (ControlNet++-style cycle consistency), but it keeps the evaluator ceiling and the condition-detector upper bound (CPO), and it does nothing for the epistemic problem.

### Gaps
- No study was found that measures YOLO, Mask2Former or OneFormer accuracy specifically on *axonometric architectural line drawings* or *archaeological reconstruction illustrations*. The sketch, clipart and PACS results are the closest proxies.
- No evidence was found on how VQA-based alignment scores (TIFA, VQAScore and the like) behave on schematic or line-art images.
- Colour-palette fidelity of SD/SDXL VAEs under ControlNet-seg conditioning was not quantified in any source found. It needs a pilot experiment.

## Q4. Perceptual/communication evidence: do viewers read photorealistic reconstructions as more certain or trustworthy than warranted?

### Takeaway
No controlled museum or visitor study was found that directly compares photoreal and schematic archaeological reconstructions on perceived *certainty*. The indirect evidence points one way. Architects choose sketches to signal a preliminary or incomplete status and are more willing to modify them (Schumann et al. 1996, N=54). Viewers rate photorealistic historic architecture as the most "accurate" (Tang et al. 2018, N=150). Even uninformative photographs inflate belief in claims (Newman et al. 2012). Realistic AI images increase the credibility of misinformation (CHI EA 2025, abstract-level). One 2026 VR study found that visual authenticity cues improved clarity but did *not* change trust, which suggests style alone is not enough without an explicit legend and paradata.

### Cited Findings
- **Schumann, Strothotte, Raab & Laser (CHI '96), "Assessing the Effect of Non-Photorealistic Rendered Images in CAD" [primary HTML, read]:** 54 architects (36% return) compared a CAD wireframe plot, a shaded rendering and a pencil-style sketch rendering of the same model. For a **first draft**, 53% chose the sketch (33% plot, 22% shaded), saying it "best shows the preliminary character of the draft". For a **final presentation**, only 8% chose the sketch (50% plot, 42% shaded; p<0.05), because committees "want to see exact renditions". Sketches "encourage discourse about a design", and drawing directly on the image was chosen for 69% of sketches vs 33% of shaded images (p<0.05). Sketches are "more readily accepted as being incomplete representations". The authors caution: "Care must be taken not to overgeneralize the results." — [Schumann et al. 1996](https://www.chi1996.chistatic.hosting.acm.org/proceedings/papers/Schumann/chi96fi.html)
- Isenberg's 2005 NPR survey describes Schumann et al. as the first evaluation of NPR in architecture and design. **[snippet]** — [Isenberg 2005](https://www.lri.fr/~isenberg/publications/papers/Isenberg_2005_NPR.pdf)
- **Tang, Adamo, Villani & Dib (2018), "Rendering style and viewer's perception of historic virtual architecture", EAI Endorsed Trans. Creative Technologies 5(16), DOI 10.4108/eai.7-3-2019.156772:** 150 subjects rated photorealistic, watercolour-NPR and technical-illustration-NPR renderings of Chinese, Gothic and Greek landmarks. "Photorealistic rendering was rated highest for visual appeal, suitability, and accuracy of representation", while "recognition of the architectural style was highest for the non-photorealistic watercolor renderings." **[abstract; significance values not seen]** — [EAI 2018](https://publications.eai.eu/index.php/ct/article/view/1495)
- **Newman, Garry, Bernstein, Kantner & Lindsay (2012), "Nonprobative photographs (or words) inflate truthiness", Psychonomic Bulletin & Review 19:969–974:** an uninformative related photo increased judgements that claims were true. Verbal information had a similar effect. — [Newman et al. 2012 full text](https://www.lifespancognition.com/s/newman-etal-truthiness-pbr12-fulltext.pdf). The effect depends on context: it appeared within-subjects but not between-subjects. — [Newman et al. 2015](https://www.lifespancognition.com/s/NewmanGarryUnkelbach-etal-TruthFalsiness-JEPLMC15.pdf)
- "Too Good to be False: How Photorealism Promotes Susceptibility to Misinformation" (ACM CHI EA 2025): highly realistic AI-generated images significantly increased the credibility of misinformation compared with less realistic images. **[search snippet only; page returned 403; design and N not verified]** — [ACM DL 10.1145/3706599.3719796](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3706599.3719796)
- Kamali et al. (CHI 2025), "Characterizing Photorealism and Artifacts in Diffusion Model-Generated Images": 749,828 observations from 50,444 participants judging real vs AI images. Detection accuracy depends on scene complexity, artifact type, display time and human curation. **[abstract]** This supports the risk that photoreal AI reconstructions may be mistaken for photographs. — [arXiv 2502.11989](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.11989v1)
- "Visualising Authenticity in Historical Virtual Reality Environments" (ACM, ~2026): visual cues improved clarity across styles but "did not affect perceived trust in reconstruction accuracy". Familiarity with local landmarks raised credibility, and mismatches with memory lowered it. **[snippet; authors and N not verified; page returned 403]** — [ACM DL 10.1145/3797993.3798021](https://dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3797993.3798021)
- A 2026 Heritage Science SEM study reports that visitors facing photorealistic scenes check provenance and compare details with prior knowledge, probing "gaps between technical realism and historical/cultural authenticity". It is not a certainty-manipulation experiment. **[snippet]** — [npj Heritage Science 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s40494-026-02345-6)
- Sifniotis et al. (VAST 2007) surveyed archaeologists, both Roman and non-Roman specialists, on which evidence types raise their confidence, and found some evidence types are favoured. This is expert-opinion input for uncertainty encoding, not a perception test of renderings. **[abstract]** — [Sifniotis et al. 2007](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.2312/VAST.VAST07.079-085); APGV 2010 poster on 3D visualisation of archaeological uncertainty (results not available) — [Sussex figshare](https://sussex.figshare.com/articles/presentation/3D_visualization_of_archaeological_uncertainty/23348726)

### Inferences
- Tang et al. found photorealism rated "most accurate" even though accuracy was not manipulated. Combined with Newman's truthiness effect and the CHI EA 2025 result, this supports the hypothesis that photoreal output inflates perceived certainty. For a hypothetical, LLM-mediated reconstruction, that inflation is unearned by construction.
- Schumann et al. show that sketch-like rendering conveys "preliminary / incomplete" status and invites correction. That is the communicative stance a hypothesis-generating pipeline should take, and it supports Seville 4.4.1 on alternative hypotheses.
- The 2026 VR finding (cues → clarity but not trust) argues for making uncertainty *explicit* (legend, evidence classes, per-element paradata) rather than relying on style alone.

### Gaps
- No controlled study was found that manipulates rendering style (photoreal vs schematic) for the *same archaeological reconstruction* and measures perceived certainty or trust in lay or expert audiences. The Tang et al. stimuli were extant landmarks, not uncertain reconstructions.
- Full texts of the CHI EA 2025 and ACM 2026 studies could not be accessed (403).
- The withdrawn arXiv paper "Pre/Absence" (2510.07967) was checked and is not relevant (VR vs paper; withdrawn).

## Q5. Synthesis: which output style should the team commit to, and how does it score on the three axes?

### Takeaway
**Recommendation: commit to a schematic output, specifically an *evidence-coded axonometric (or plan-view) reconstruction schematic*.** This means clean line-art in parallel projection, flat palette-locked semantic fills per layout class, and line style or saturation encoding each element's evidence status. Use photorealistic rendering only as an internal **ablation arm**, not as the deliverable. This is the only option that scores well on epistemic validity and CV measurability together. It costs some feasibility (style LoRA, custom evaluator), which the procedurally generated paired data mitigates.

### Cited Findings
- The decision itself is mandated to be explicit and documented: London Charter 2.3 (photo-realistic / impressionistic / schematic choice), 4.4 (state the extent and nature of factual uncertainty), 4.6 (paradata) and 4.7 (rationale for choosing and rejecting methods). — [London Charter 2.1](https://www.londoncharter.org/fileadmin/templates/main/docs/london_charter_2_1_en.pdf)
- Seville 4.4.2 and 4.4.3 require visible levels of accuracy and a distinction between in situ, anastylosis, rebuilt and virtual. 8.4 says quality is measured "by the rigour … and not the spectacularity". — [López-Menchero & Grande 2011](https://www.cipaheritagedocumentation.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/12/López-Menchero-Grande-The-principles-of-the-Seville-Charter.pdf)
- Photorealism "suggests that a camera could have taken the picture". — [Strothotte et al. 1999](https://tobias.isenberg.cc/personal/papers/Strothotte_1999_VKV.pdf)
- Off-the-shelf evaluators lose ~50% of AP on non-photo domains (26.8 vs 55.4 mAP on Clipart1k; 0.26 vs 0.74 AP for Grounding DINO on sketches). — [Inoue et al. 2018](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1803.11365); [Tang et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.09608v2)
- Photoreal evaluators are capped too: 0.58 IoU on ground-truth ADE20K images. — [ControlNet](https://arxiv.org/html/2302.05543v3)
- Domain-specific detectors trained on synthetic renders are an accepted protocol (LayoutBench). — [LayoutBench](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2304.06671)
- ControlNet-seg accepts manual colour-coded masks, and lineart/MLSD conditioning exists. — [ControlNet 1.1](https://github.com/lllyasviel/ControlNet-v1-1-nightly)
- Diffusion can produce publication-standard archaeological drawings after fine-tuning. — [PyPotteryInk](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.06897)

### Inferences

**Recommended specification ("evidence-coded schematic")**
1. **Projection:** plan view (most measurable; matches excavation and phase plans) or isometric/axonometric (the classic reconstruction drawing). Parallel projection keeps the layout-to-image mapping affine, so ground truth can be projected exactly.
2. **Semantic channel:** each layout class (wall, floor, hearth, pit, posthole, doorway, room interior, etc.) gets a flat fill from a fixed, well-separated palette. Pass it as the ControlNet-seg condition plus GLIGEN boxes for discrete objects.
3. **Evidence channel (paradata made visible):** solid outline and full saturation for elements attested in the report as excavated or in situ; dashed outline or reduced saturation for elements inferred by analogy or physical constraint; dotted outline or ghosting for hypothetical elements. These map onto Seville 4.4.3 and onto Strothotte's "reason" categories. Store the source sentence or span from the report and the evidence class per element (London 4.6).
4. **Style:** a LoRA trained mainly on procedurally rendered schematic images of synthetic layouts, which gives paired, copyright-clean data, plus a small set of licensed or public-domain reconstruction drawings.
5. **Evaluation stack:**
   - (a) Palette-quantisation mIoU and connected-component box mAP/IoU against the projected LLM layout.
   - (b) Lineart/MLSD re-extraction SSIM/F1 against the condition edges (ControlNet++ protocol).
   - (c) A detector fine-tuned on synthetic schematic renders (LayoutBench protocol), as a learned cross-check.
   - (d) All metrics also run on ground-truth renders, to calibrate evaluator error.
   - (e) A "hallucination area" metric: palette or ink pixels outside every layout element.
   - (f) CLIP/VQA only as auxiliary semantic checks.
   - (g) A small expert or end-user rating of legibility and uncertainty communication (Seville 8.3).
6. **Ablation arm:** a photorealistic render of the same layouts, evaluated with off-the-shelf YOLO/OneFormer and Grounding DINO. This gives comparability with GLIGEN and ControlNet numbers and quantifies the domain-gap argument empirically. Label every photoreal image "hypothetical reconstruction" per London 4.4.

**Scoring table (1 = poor, 5 = strong)**

| Option | Epistemic validity | Technical feasibility | CV measurability | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. Photorealistic (deliverable) | 2 | 4 | 3 | 9 |
| B. Plain schematic line-art (no semantic or evidence coding), off-the-shelf evaluators | 4 | 3 | 2 | 9 |
| C. Photoreal generation, evaluated via edge/seg re-extraction (hybrid-eval) | 2 | 4 | 3 | 9 |
| **D. Evidence-coded schematic (recommended), with photoreal ablation arm** | **5** | **3** | **4** | **12** |

**Reasoning per score**
- **A, Epistemic 2:** it can satisfy London 4.4 only through overlays that break photorealism. It suggests camera-like certainty (Strothotte 1999), is rated "most accurate" without justification (Tang 2018), and is the regime where AI priors inject outdated or biased content that viewers cannot separate from evidence (Magnani & Clindaniel 2026; Morgan 2025). Not 1, because the Charter does allow photorealism if justified and documented, and ghosting or colour-tint conventions exist.
- **A, Feasibility 4:** it is the native regime of ControlNet-seg (ADE20K/COCO) and GLIGEN (COCO) and needs no style training. Not 5, because archaeological classes are out of vocabulary and DALL-E 2 photoreal prompts failed for archaeological scenes (Magnani & Clindaniel 2023).
- **A, Measurability 3:** standard protocols exist and are comparable, but ground-truth photos cap at 0.58 IoU, archaeological classes are absent from COCO and ADE20K, and out-of-distribution layouts collapse AP (LayoutBench: 54.5 → 9.9).
- **B, Epistemic 4:** follows illustration convention and signals hypothesis (Schumann 1996; Lengyel & Toulouse), but without explicit evidence coding it does not meet Seville 4.4.2 "levels of accuracy".
- **B, Feasibility 3:** lineart/MLSD ControlNets and architecture LoRAs exist, but there is no public training corpus and line drawings are prone to structural errors (Text2CAD, SciFlow-Bench, LineArt).
- **B, Measurability 2:** photo-trained detectors lose about half their AP on sketch and clipart domains (Inoue; PACS; Tang et al. 2025), so generator errors cannot be separated from evaluator errors.
- **C:** inherits A's epistemic score and A's evaluator ceiling. Edge-based SSIM adds a style-agnostic signal but is bounded by the condition detector (CPO).
- **D, Epistemic 5:** implements London 4.4, 4.6 and 4.10 and Seville 4.4.2 and 4.4.3 directly in the image, matches the reconstruction-drawing tradition, and narrows the generator's freedom to hallucinate. One residual concern is the Seville 4.5.3 "lifeless" critique; it can be addressed by depicting phases and states of decay.
- **D, Feasibility 3:** needs a style LoRA, a palette-fidelity pilot and a procedural renderer. All are standard course-scale work. Paired synthetic data removes the data-scarcity problem, and PyPotteryInk shows diffusion can reach publication-grade archaeological line work.
- **D, Measurability 4:** colour-quantisation mIoU and connected-component boxes need no learned evaluator. LayoutBench-style detector fine-tuning has strong precedent (0.26 → 0.74 AP). Not 5, because of the copy-through circularity risk and the untested colour drift of the VAE.

### Gaps
- No direct evidence yet on (i) palette colour fidelity of SD/SDXL + ControlNet-seg, (ii) how often a schematic LoRA produces broken or open lines under layout conditioning, or (iii) whether lay viewers read the evidence-coding legend correctly. All three should be pilot-tested early (e.g., 50 layouts, both arms).
- The recommendation optimises for a research and evaluation setting. If the end audience were the public in a museum, Seville 8.2 calls for visitor studies, and a more pictorial, but still coded, style might be justified.
- The ground truth for layout faithfulness is the LLM's layout, not the excavated reality. The text-to-layout step needs its own evaluation (e.g., element recall against report annotations) to satisfy London 4.6 and Seville Principle 7 end to end.
