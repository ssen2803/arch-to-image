# Generative AI, NLP and Computer Vision in Archaeology & Cultural Heritage — inventory for the gap statement (text report -> LLM layout -> layout-conditioned diffusion -> CV evaluation)

Scope note: compiled 2026-11-14 from web search plus fetches of primary pages where reachable. Many publisher pages (MDPI, ScienceDirect, ACM DL, Eurographics DL) returned HTTP 403, so some details come from search-engine abstracts and snippets. These are marked "(snippet)" or "unverified". Items flagged **[2026]** are the most recent. Pipeline-stage codes: **T** = text input, **L** = explicit spatial layout (as conditioning or output), **G** = generative model (diffusion/GAN/T2I/LLM), **E** = quantitative CV evaluation, **A** = real archaeological/heritage data.

## Q1. Diffusion / GAN / text-to-image for archaeological or heritage reconstruction (and which use spatial conditioning)

### Takeaway
Generative restoration work in heritage is extensive. The densest strands are mural inpainting (Dunhuang and other Chinese murals) and pottery and 3D artefact completion, and spatial conditioning (ControlNet, line drawings, edge maps, wireframes, segmentation) is common there. None of the works found conditions generation on a layout derived from excavation text. Site- and building-scale works condition on photos, edges or wireframes, or prompt with free text, and they are mostly evaluated visually or by experts rather than with quantitative CV metrics.

### Cited Findings

**Murals / Dunhuang (image inpainting; spatial conditioning common)**
- *DiffuMural: Restoring Dunhuang Murals with Multi-scale Diffusion*, arXiv:2504.09513 (Apr 2025). It combines multi-scale collaborative diffusion with **ControlNet** and a cyclic-consistency loss, targeting large damaged areas with few training samples. It is evaluated on **23 authentic Dunhuang murals**, and on regular-size restoration it reports SSIM/PSNR gains of 14.61% and 4.73% over the best of four CNN baselines. It also proposes a four-dimensional evaluation metric that includes a "humanistic value" dimension. Stages: G, L (ControlNet structure), E, A. Text input: none. — [arXiv 2504.09513](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09513v1) (numbers are the authors' own and come from the snippet)
- A journal paper titled *DiffuMural: a diffusion model for Dunhuang murals restoration via multi-scale convergence and cooperative guidance* appears in *Heritage Science* (2025). I did not verify whether it is the journal version of the arXiv paper. — [Heritage Science s40494-025-02274-w](https://www.nature.com/articles/s40494-025-02274-w)
- A RePaint diffusion model pre-trained on a public dataset and fine-tuned on **5,135 Dunhuang mural images** reports better PSNR/SSIM than recent restoration models (ACM conference paper, DOI 10.1145/3614008.3614024). Stages: G, E, A. — [Unpaywall record](https://unpaywall.org/10.1145%2F3614008.3614024) (snippet)
- A structure-guided SDE-based diffusion model for the Dunhuang dataset adds a structure-reconstruction module before diffusion (*Journal of Beijing University of Posts and Telecommunications*, 2024, in Chinese). — [JBUPT PDF](https://castjournals.cast.org.cn/joweb/bjyddxxb/EN/PDF/10.13190/j.jbupt.2024-174) (snippet)
- *DiffInpaint: line drawing guided murals restoration with diffusion model* (*Measurement*, 2025). It uses **line drawings as shape guidance** for a latent diffusion model and releases a Dunhuang mural image/line-drawing pair dataset. **Mosaics and frescoes**
- Moral-Andrés, Merino-Gómez, Reviriego & Lombardi, *Can Artificial Intelligence Reconstruct Ancient Mosaics?*, arXiv:2210.06145 (Oct 2022), later published in *Studies in Conservation* 69(5), 2024. They tested **DALL-E 2 outpainting** on 8 mosaics (opus tessellatum and vermiculatum). The outputs "capture the essence" of a scene but sometimes fail on details and geometric forms or introduce inconsistent elements. Stages: G, A. Evaluation is qualitative comparison with the originals; no quantitative CV metric was found. — [arXiv 2210.06145](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.06145)
- Tsesmelis et al. (22 authors), *Re-assembling the past: The RePAIR dataset and benchmark for real world 2D and 3D puzzle solving*, NeurIPS 2024 Datasets & Benchmarks, arXiv:2410.24010. The data are fragments of a Pompeii fresco that collapsed in a WWII bombing: of 16,000 pieces, 1,000 were puzzle-solved manually by archaeologists to give ground truth. It provides 2D PNGs with GT transforms and 3D OBJ meshes, plus standardized reassembly metrics, and the baselines show "a gap to fill". It is a reassembly benchmark, not a generative one, but it is a real-data quantitative benchmark. The paper is also listed in the Israeli CRIS portal, which indicates an Israeli co-author institution. — [arXiv 2410.24010](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.24010v2); [Zenodo 13993089](https://zenodo.org/records/13993089); [IUCC CRIS](https://cris.iucc.ac.il/en/publications/re-assembling-the-past-the-repair-dataset-and-benchmark-for-real--2/)

**Pottery, artefacts, 3D**
- *Reconstruction of Iberian ceramic potteries using generative adversarial networks* (*Scientific Reports*, 2022; University of Jaén group). The customized GAN (IberianGAN) is trained on **1,072 wheel-made Iberian pottery profiles** and reconstructs full 2D profiles from rim or base fragments. It is assessed quantitatively and qualitatively, including by archaeologists. Stages: G, E, A. — [Scientific Reports s41598-022-14910-7](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-022-14910-7); [PMC9225991](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9225991/)
- *IberianVoxel* (IJCAI 2023) is a 3D autoencoder-GAN trained on 1,001 voxelized pottery models, each fragmented into multiple pieces. — [UJaén repository](https://rujapre.ujaen.es/items/f74c5bf8-8a25-449b-bbcc-82a5f2b668a3) (snippet; I did not verify that this repository item is IberianVoxel itself rather than IberianGAN)
- Lorenzo Cardarelli, *PyPotteryInk: One-Step Diffusion Model for Sketch to Publication-ready Archaeological Drawings*, arXiv:2502.06897 (9 Feb 2025). A modified img2img-turbo diffusion model, applied patch-based, turns pencil sketches into inked drawings. It was tested on Italian protohistoric pottery and validated by expert evaluation, cutting time from hours to seconds per drawing. This is **sketch-conditioned diffusion on real archaeological drawings**. Stages: G, L (sketch as condition), A. — [arXiv 2502.06897](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.06897v1)
- *Image processing for graphic normalisation of the ceramic profile in archaeological sketches making use of deep neuronal net (DNN)* (*DAACH*, 2021). It detects the profile region, normalizes it with GANs and builds a 3D volumetric model. — [ScienceDirect S2212054821000254](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2212054821000254)
- *3D Reconstruction of Incomplete Archaeological Objects Using a Generative Adversarial Network*, arXiv:1711.06363 (voxel 3D GAN completion). — [arXiv 1711.06363](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1711.06363)
- *Cultural Heritage 3D Reconstruction with Diffusion Networks*, arXiv:2410.10927 (2024), also published as a Springer chapter (10.1007/978-3-031-91572-7_7; PUCP, Peru). A conditional diffusion model reconstructs point clouds of damaged artefacts, with data diversity and outlier sensitivity reported as challenges. — [arXiv 2410.10927](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10927)
- *CPDC-MFNet: conditional point diffusion completion network with multi-scale feedback refine for 3D Terracotta Warriors* (*Scientific Reports*, 2024). — [PMC11004119](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11004119/)
- *StatueDiff: Regionally Enhanced Cross-Scale Diffusion* for single-view 3D reconstruction of sculptural heritage (ISPRS Archives XLVIII-M-9-2025). — [ISPRS PDF](https://isprs-archives.copernicus.org/articles/XLVIII-M-9-2025/429/2025/isprs-archives-XLVIII-M-9-2025-429-2025.pdf)
- **[2026]** A GAN with self-attention for heritage point-cloud completion notes that existing completion methods, trained mostly on synthetic CAD data, "have not been specifically validated for cultural heritage artifacts". — [Springer s10791-026-10049-5](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10791-026-10049-5)

A diffusion model fine-tuned on museum and open-access images of Harappan pottery, seals and figurines predicts original colours, and each reconstruction is documented with ethical and provenance metadata. — [ScienceDirect S2212054826000561](https://sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2212054826000561) (snippet; page returned 403)

### Inferences
- Spatial conditioning in heritage diffusion mainly takes the form of **line drawings, edge maps, wireframes and segmentation masks derived from images**. No surveyed work conditions on a **symbolic layout** (boxes or polygons of loci, walls and installations) derived from text.
- Quantitative CV metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) dominate the mural and artefact inpainting strands because ground truth or masked originals exist. Site- and building-scale generation has no ground-truth image of the ancient appearance, so evaluation falls back to experts. Evaluating geometry with CV (detectors or segmenters checking whether the generated image respects the specified layout) would therefore be novel in this domain.
- The Heritage review's figure of 13 genAI reconstruction papers out of more than 8,700 indicates that site- and building-scale generative reconstruction is still a thin literature.

### Gaps
- Full texts of DiffInpaint, GuidePaint, Kizil, Jiangnan and the Harappan paper were not readable (403), so their exact metrics and author lists are unverified.
- I could not confirm the authors of ARQGAN, IberianGAN or the arXiv 1711.06363 paper from primary pages, so author names are omitted rather than guessed.
- I found no paper applying ControlNet or layout-to-image diffusion to **excavated ruins conditioned on an excavation plan**. This absence is from searches of my own and not exhaustive.

## Q2. Text-to-image / LLM-driven visualization of the past and critical studies of "hallucinated" pasts

### Takeaway
A clear critical literature exists, centred on *Advances in Archaeological Practice*, and it documents bias, outdated science and gibberish plans in DALL-E, Midjourney and ChatGPT outputs. Work that generates site images from historical texts is anecdotal or prompt-based, with no structured intermediate representation and no quantitative CV evaluation.

### Cited Findings
- Peter J. Cobb, *Large Language Models and Generative AI, Oh My! Archaeology in the Time of ChatGPT, Midjourney, and Beyond*, *Advances in Archaeological Practice* 11(3):363–369 (2023), DOI 10.1017/aap.2023.20 (Digital Review). Cobb queried chatbots with archaeological questions over several months, and a DALL-E 2 "site plan" image "might look like a plan from far away but is actually gibberish". This is direct evidence that unconditioned T2I fails at archaeological layout. — [HKU repository](https://repository.hku.hk/handle/10722/333897); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/374090951_Large_Language_Models_and_Generative_AI_Oh_My_Archaeology_in_the_Time_of_ChatGPT_Midjourney_and_Beyond)
- Matthew Magnani & Jon Clindaniel, *Artificial Intelligence and Archaeological Illustration*, *Advances in Archaeological Practice* (2023; Cambridge PII S2326376823000256, so the DOI is probably 10.1017/aap.2023.25, inferred from the PII and unverified). They use DALL-E 2 to illustrate competing Neanderthal hypotheses and argue that AI "promises to democratize" visual representations of the past. — [Cambridge Core](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/advances-in-archaeological-practice/article/artificial-intelligence-and-archaeological-illustration/253B777A512E28573AACEAC6977EDCB4)
- **[2026]** Magnani & Clindaniel et al., *Artificial Intelligence and the Interpretation of the Past*, *Advances in Archaeological Practice* 14:218–233 (2026; published online Dec 2025), DOI 10.1017/aap.2025.10110, CC BY 4.0. The authors call for AI-accessible scholarly datasets. — [EurekAlert release](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1115630); [Cambridge PDF](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/8FE3F2CB6BBFAD49F75FFC3031158A5A/S2326376825101101a.pdf/artificial_intelligence_and_the_interpretation_of_the_past.pdf). ### Inferences
- The critical literature supports the project's motivation: free-text prompting of T2I models gives plausible-looking but spatially and historically wrong images (Cobb's gibberish plan; Maurice's Roman Britain). An explicit layout intermediate is a principled response to that, though none of these papers proposes it.
- Evaluation in this cluster is content analysis, expert review or student rating. None uses detectors or segmenters to check fidelity to a specification.

- I found no *Antiquity* or *Internet Archaeology* article specifically on T2I depictions of the past. Targeted searches returned other journals, so this is unconfirmed rather than ruled out.
- The exact author list of the 2026 AAP paper is unverified because the PDF body was unreadable.

## Q3. NLP / information extraction from excavation reports and grey literature (including LLMs and any spatial-layout extraction)

### Takeaway
There is a mature NER and semantic-indexing tradition: the UK (ADS/OASIS with STAR/STELLAR/OPTIMA mapped to CIDOC-CRM), the Netherlands (AGNES/ArcheoBERTje), multilingual archaeology BERTs and Chinese site-text IE. An emerging LLM strand (2025–26) maps archaeological data to CIDOC-CRM RDF. It extracts entities (artefacts, features, materials, periods, places) and coarse spatio-temporal facts, but **no work extracts geometric spatial layout** (positions, dimensions, adjacency of loci, walls and installations) from report text.

### Cited Findings
**UK / ADS / CIDOC-CRM**
- Richards, Tudhope & Vlachidis, "Text Mining in Archaeology: Extracting Information from Archaeological Reports" (2015 book chapter). It reports the ADS grey-literature library (collected via **OASIS**) at more than 26,000 reports in June 2014, growing by about 200 a month, and places the work within the Archaeotools, STAR and STELLAR projects. — [White Rose eprint](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/86788/1/Text_Mining_in_Archaeology.pdf)

**Spatial information extraction (closest to "layout from text")**
- Yuan et al., *Extracting Spatio-Temporal Information from Chinese Archaeological Site Text*, *ISPRS IJGI* 11(3):175 (2022). Deep-learning NER plus relation extraction (site name, location, culture, dynasty, "location of site" relations) is stored in a Neo4j knowledge graph. "Spatial" here means **site location and coordinates, not intra-site layout**. — [MDPI IJGI](https://www.mdpi.com/2220-9964/11/3/175)
- "Spatiotemporal data as the foundation of an archaeological stratigraphy extraction and management system" models stratigraphic units as 2D polygons with top-down spatial relations, but from excavation data, not text. — [ResearchGate 288686826](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/288686826_Spatiotemporal_data_as_the_foundation_of_an_archaeological_stratigraphy_extraction_and_management_system)
- *Ontological Modeling for Excavation Documentation and Virtual Reconstruction of an Ancient Egyptian Site*, *ACM JOCCH* (2021), DOI 10.1145/3439735. It links excavation documentation, through an ontology, to virtual reconstruction. I could not read it (403), so whether it involves NLP or ML, or encodes wall and room geometry, is unverified. It is likely the closest conceptual precursor of "report to structured spatial model to visualization". — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3439735)
- Non-archaeological analogs for the LLM-to-layout stage: *HouseTune: Two-Stage Floorplan Generation with LLM Assistance*, where an LLM with chain-of-thought drafts a layout and a diffusion model refines it, arXiv:2411.12279 — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.12279); **[2026]** *HypergraphFormer: Learning Hypergraphs from LLMs for Editable Floor Plan Generation*, arXiv:2605.18932 — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.18932)
- General LLM-extraction accuracy on grey literature (non-archaeological): GPT-4's extraction sensitivity was 0.65 on grey literature versus 0.75 on English peer-reviewed text (*Research Synthesis Methods*, 2024). — [arXiv 2310.17526](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.17526v2)

### Inferences
- The entity schemas used so far (artefact, feature, material, period, place; CRM classes) capture **what** and **when**, not **where within the site** or **how large**. A schema covering locus, wall, installation, coordinates or orientation, dimensions and adjacency, with an LLM extracting it into a layout JSON, appears unaddressed for archaeological reports.
- The CIDOC-CRM LLM work (Hariri et al.) is the nearest methodological precedent for ontology-constrained structured extraction. Its finding that a subset of the ontology plus few-shot examples works best transfers directly to a layout schema.

### Gaps
- I found no LLM evaluation (precision/recall) on excavation reports for stratigraphy, dimensions or spatial relations. The Electronics review claims such uses, but I could not verify the primary studies behind it.
- I found no Swedish archaeological-report NER work. SWENER-1800 is historical Swedish, not archaeology. Italian archaeological-report NER was not found either.
- Archaeotools (the UK project) is named in the Richards et al. chapter, but I did not retrieve its own primary publication.

## Q4. Computer vision in archaeology usable as evaluators, and datasets of digitized plans and drawings

### Takeaway
Mature detectors and segmenters exist for LiDAR and aerial features, pottery, and (recently) stones and wall segments in drone imagery. Historical floor-plan wall-segmentation datasets exist (Versailles-FP), and pottery-drawing digitization tools exist. **No public dataset of digitized excavation plans with wall/locus annotations was found**, which is both a gap and a practical obstacle for evaluation.

### Cited Findings
- **[2026]** Erel Uziel, Motti Zohar & Yitzchak Jaffe (University of Haifa), *Semi-automatic detection of building stones and wall segments of archaeological ruins*, *Journal of Archaeological Science* 185:106430 (2026, online 2025). Two deep models, one for stones and one for wall segments, run on drone imagery and output a georeferenced **stones layer and a site-plan layer of wall segments**. It was tested on nine sites, with open-source models and code. This is a strong candidate CV evaluator for wall layout in generated images, and it comes from an Israeli group. — [Haifa-hosted PDF](https://mzohar.haifa.ac.il/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/Uziel-et-al-2025-Semi-automatic-detection-of-building-stones_JAS.pdf); [ScienceDirect S0305440325002791](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0305440325002791). The volume and article number come from the search snippet, the PDF was too large to fetch, and the site names and metrics are unverified. A Jerusalem Post piece reports about 350,000 stones detected (about 20% in walls) in work linked to Garfinkel and Hasel, but I could not confirm that it describes this paper. — [JPost](https://www.jpost.com/archaeology/article-880663)
- *Versailles-FP dataset: Wall Detection in Ancient Floor Plans*, arXiv:2103.08064: 500 floor-plan images from 17th–18th-century Versailles with wall-mask ground truth. Walls are easily confused with other lines, the same problem scanned excavation plans present. — [arXiv 2103.08064](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.08064)
- *Deep Vectorization of Technical Drawings* (ECCV 2020), arXiv:2003.05471: CNN cleaning, then per-patch primitive estimation, then refinement. It is general rather than archaeological, but could vectorize excavation plans. — [arXiv 2003.05471](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.05471)
- Verschoof-van der Vaart & Lambers, *Learning to Look at LiDAR: The Use of R-CNN in the Automated Detection of Archaeological Objects in LiDAR Data from the Netherlands*, *JCAA* 2(1):31–40 (2019) (WODAN). WODAN 2.0 (*ISPRS IJGI* 9(5):293, 2020, with Kowalczyk and Bourgeois) reports about 70% for barrows and Celtic fields on a small test set, falling to about 50%, 46% and 18% (barrows, Celtic fields, charcoal kilns) on a large random set. — [Augsburg repository PDF](https://opus.bibliothek.uni-augsburg.de/opus4/files/124229/124229.pdf); [IJGI mirror](https://p.gyb.elementfx.com/2220-9964/9/5/293) (snippet)
- *Archaeoscape: Bringing Aerial Laser Scanning Archaeology to the Deep Learning Era* (NeurIPS 2024 Datasets & Benchmarks): the largest open ALS archaeology dataset, covering **888 km²** with **31,411 annotated** anthropogenic features. — [NeurIPS PDF](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/2d9ba88c62949a9cef5c056ee77f2366-Paper-Datasets_and_Benchmarks_Track.pdf)
- **[2026]** *Automatic detection of archaeological features from airborne laser scanning data using deep learning: Development and evaluation of the ADAF tool* (*JAS: Reports*, 2026). — [ScienceDirect S2352409X26001689](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2352409X26001689)
- *Combined Detection and Segmentation of Archeological Structures from LiDAR Data Using a Deep Learning Approach* (*JCAA*): Neolithic structures, at most 110 training images, mAP up to 0.77. — [JCAA 10.5334/jcaa.64](https://journal.caa-international.org/articles/10.5334/jcaa.64)
- *Transfer Learning of Semantic Segmentation Methods for Identifying Buried Archaeological Structures on LiDAR Data*, arXiv:2307.03512. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.03512)
- *Archaeological site segmentation of ancient city walls based on deep learning and LiDAR remote sensing* (*Journal of Cultural Heritage*, 2023/24): U-Net delineates city walls with 94.12% precision. — [ScienceDirect S1296207423002145](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1296207423002145) (snippet)
- *Potential of deep learning segmentation for the extraction of archaeological features from historical map series* (*Archaeological Prospection*, 2021): U-Net applied to whole historical map series. — [Wiley 10.1002/arp.1807](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/arp.1807); [PMC8248396](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8248396/)

- **ArchAIDE** (*Internet Archaeology* 52): shape-based and appearance-based deep classifiers for pottery, with a final dataset of **10,036** items. Tel Aviv University and CNR produced synthetic training data by building 3D models from 2D drawings and virtually breaking them. — [Internet Archaeology 52 §3](https://intarch.ac.uk/journal/issue52/7/3.html); [CORDIS](https://cordis.europa.eu/article/id/411445)

- **[2026]** *OCR-Based Field Extraction for Archaeological Pottery Metadata: The CENTURIA Dataset*, arXiv:2608.30616 (handwritten metadata on Carnuntum pottery drawings). — [Hugging Face papers](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.30616.md)

### Inferences
- For evaluating whether a generated image respects the specified layout, the most transferable tools are: (a) the Uziel et al. stone and wall-segment detector, which is trained on real Levantine-style ruins from drone views and outputs a wall layer comparable to a specified layout via IoU or Chamfer; (b) Versailles-FP-style wall segmentation for plan-view outputs; (c) generic open-vocabulary detectors and segmenters (outside this survey's scope).
- The lack of a public excavation-plan dataset means any project wanting layout ground truth would likely have to digitize plans from reports, for example HA-ESI report figures. That is my inference, not a sourced claim.

### Gaps
- I found no dataset of scanned excavation plans or section drawings with wall/locus annotations. I also found no neural work on vectorizing excavation plans specifically.

## Q6. Synthesis — closest works to the full pipeline (excavation text -> LLM structured layout -> layout-conditioned diffusion -> CV evaluation on archaeological data)

### Takeaway
**No work found combines all four stages, and none combines even stages 1+2+3 on archaeological data.** Each stage exists in isolation: text-to-structure (CIDOC-CRM, NER), image-structure-conditioned diffusion (murals, facades, sketches), and archaeological CV detectors (walls, LiDAR features). The specific missing link is **extracting an explicit geometric layout from excavation-report text and using it to condition generation**. Quantitative CV evaluation of layout fidelity in generated archaeological imagery is also absent.

### Cited Findings (closest works and what each is missing)
- **Arzomand, Rustell & Kalganova 2024** (text from historical records to Midjourney/DALL-E images of Palmyra, Chichen Itza and Pompeii). Has: text input, generation, real heritage subjects. **Missing:** an LLM-structured intermediate, any spatial layout or conditioning, excavation reports as the source, and quantitative CV evaluation (expert and comparative only). — [Brunel](https://bura.brunel.ac.uk/handle/2438/30634?mode=full)
- **Laroche et al., EG DH 2025** (Cordeliers Church: curated sources plus an Omeka-S ontology feeding generative visual hypotheses). Has: structured knowledge from historical sources, generation, expert review. **Missing (as far as visible from the snippet):** LLM extraction from excavation text, explicit layout conditioning, quantitative CV evaluation. — [Eurographics DL](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.2312/dh20253146) (unverified)
- **Vespasiano & Brusaporci 2026** (text prompts plus graphical inputs for a lost church). Has: text with spatial and graphical conditioning, heritage subject. **Missing:** automated extraction from reports, a structured layout, CV metrics (unverified). — [UnivAQ](https://ricerca.univaq.it/handle/11697/275060) (unverified)
- **Heritage Science 2025 facade framework / Xu et al. 2024 Minnan / Qing tea landscape 2025** (SD with ControlNet conditioned on wireframes, Canny edges or SAM segmentation, plus prompts). Has: spatial conditioning and text prompts on heritage architecture. **Missing:** text-derived layout (the conditions come from images or drawings), excavated or archaeological data, and quantitative layout-fidelity evaluation. — [s40494-025-01826-4](https://www.nature.com/articles/s40494-025-01826-4); [10.3390/info15060344](https://doi.org/10.3390/info15060344); [s40494-025-02183-y](https://www.nature.com/articles/s40494-025-02183-y)

Has: spatially conditioned diffusion, quantitative evaluation, real heritage data. **Missing:** text input and layout from text; the task is inpainting, not scene reconstruction. — [arXiv 2504.09513](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09513v1)
- **PyPotteryInk 2025** (sketch-conditioned one-step diffusion on real archaeological drawings). **Missing:** text input, site-scale layout, CV metrics (expert evaluation). — [arXiv 2502.06897](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.06897v1)
- **Hariri et al. 2025 / Yuan et al. 2022 / ArcheoBERTje 2022 / OPTIMA** (text to structured entities or CRM triples). Has: stage 1 on real archaeological text. **Missing:** geometric layout (positions, dimensions, adjacency), generation, CV evaluation. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-032-02049-9_6); [IJGI](https://www.mdpi.com/2220-9964/11/3/175); [arXiv 2106.07742](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.07742)

Unverified because the page returned 403. — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3439735)
- **HouseTune 2024** (LLM drafts a floor-plan layout, diffusion refines it). It has the exact stage structure but on **modern residential plans**. **Missing:** archaeological data and reports, image-level rendering of ruins or reconstructions. — [arXiv 2411.12279](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.12279)

### Inferences
- **Precise gap statement (supported by absence across all searches):** no published work (i) extracts an explicit **intra-site spatial layout** (loci, walls and installations with positions, dimensions and adjacency) from **excavation-report text** with an LLM, (ii) uses that layout to **condition a diffusion model** (layout-to-image, ControlNet or GLIGEN-style), or (iii) evaluates the outputs **quantitatively with CV models** for layout fidelity on **real archaeological data**. This holds in English and Dutch, and for Hebrew/HA-ESI in particular. The pairwise combinations that do exist are: text to structure (NLP/CRM), image-derived structure to diffusion (murals, facades), free text to T2I with expert critique, and CV detectors without generation.
- The nearest pairwise neighbours for positioning are Arzomand et al. 2024 (text to T2I for ruins), DiffuMural and the facade/Minnan ControlNet works (structure-conditioned diffusion with metrics), Hariri et al. 2025 (LLM to structured archaeological KG), Uziel et al. 2026 (archaeological wall-layout CV), and HouseTune (LLM to layout to diffusion outside archaeology).

### Gaps
- The absence claims rest on English-language web search plus fetches of primary pages where reachable. Hebrew-script sources, Chinese-language journals (an active field for heritage diffusion), CAA conference proceedings and Google Scholar full-text search were not exhaustively covered. A final check of Google Scholar for "excavation report" with "layout" and "diffusion", and of the CAA 2025/2026 programmes, is advisable before the gap is stated publicly.
- Several items' metrics and author lists are unverified (marked "?" or "(snippet)") because publisher pages returned 403.
